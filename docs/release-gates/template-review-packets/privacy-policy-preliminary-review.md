## Preliminary text-to-source crosswalk

These points summarize source text and identify review questions; they do not decide applicability or what the template must say.

| Source text | Current draft coverage | Question for Moroccan counsel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Law 09-08, Article 5(1)(a)-(b): identity of controller/representative and processing purposes | Sections 2 and 4 contain organization and purpose placeholders | Is this privacy policy the right notice instrument for the actual collection flows, or must the information also appear at each point of collection? |
| Article 5(1)(c): additional information such as recipients; whether responses are mandatory/optional and consequences of non-response; access and rectification where necessary for fair processing | Sections 5 and 8 cover recipients and a generic rights process; there are no fields for mandatory/optional responses or consequences | Which of these items are necessary for each actual form or flow, and should the template add scoped placeholders rather than blanket text? |
| Article 5(1)(d): characteristics of CNDP declaration receipt or authorization | No dedicated field | What information, if any, belongs in this notice for the controller's actual processing and applicable CNDP process? |
| Article 5(2): collection documents are to contain the preceding information | The draft is a general notice, with no form-level placement directions | Which collection forms, account flows, or interfaces need the information directly, and how should the notice refer to them? |
| Article 5(3): when data are not collected from the person, specified information is to be provided before recording or, where third-party disclosure is planned, no later than first disclosure, subject to the stated exception | Section 3 asks for data sources but does not specify a timing workflow for indirectly obtained data | Should an indirect-collection placeholder and timing instruction be added, and what exceptions or fact patterns apply? |
| Article 5(4): information about possible circulation/read/use over open networks, subject to the person's existing knowledge | No dedicated field | Does this apply to the actual service and collection channel, and if so what accurate wording is appropriate? |
| Articles 3(1)(b),(e): specified legitimate purposes and retention no longer than necessary for those purposes | Sections 4 and 7 ask for purposes and actual retention periods/criteria | Confirm how each purpose maps to collection/use and how retention criteria can be stated accurately without inventing fixed legal periods. |
| Article 4: consent rule and enumerated situations where consent is not required | Section 4 asks counsel to explain grounds per actual purpose and warns against defaulting to consent | Confirm the legal terminology and whether a “legal grounds” summary is useful in this notice; do not import GDPR categories or imply a single default ground. |
| Articles 7–10: access, rectification, objection, and direct-marketing provisions, with conditions and limits in the text | Section 8 leaves applicable rights/process to be completed after review | Identify the rights and procedures applicable to this service; do not turn the template into a generic rights list or promise operational deadlines without checking the text and actual process. |
| Articles 43–44: conditions for transfers of personal data abroad | Section 6 requires a verified account of external access/transfers and expressly makes no legality claim | Map real hosting, support, and vendor access; assess destination and applicable CNDP process. Do not state “permitted,” “approved,” or “exempt” without case-specific review. |
