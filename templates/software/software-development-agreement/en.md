## 2. Development process and changes

The parties should document their working method, decision-makers, review cadence, development environment, and change-control process in the SOW. A requested change to scope, timing, or price should be recorded for review; this discussion draft does not itself approve a change or set a binding change procedure. <!-- section: responsibilities -->
## 3. Inputs and responsibilities

The SOW should list client-provided specifications, content, credentials, equipment, access, and timely decisions, together with provider assumptions and dependencies. The parties should identify who may approve work and how blockers are escalated. Do not include production credentials, personal information, or confidential client data in a public repository or review packet.

<!-- section: delivery -->
## 4. Testing, delivery, and acceptance

The SOW should specify test environments, test cases, delivery format, review contacts, defect handling, and any acceptance steps the parties intend to use. Counsel should review any deadline, deemed acceptance, warranty, remedy, or payment consequence tied to acceptance. This draft sets no acceptance deadline and does not make delivery or silence equivalent to acceptance.

<!-- section: fees -->
## 5. Fees and expenses

The SOW should state the agreed price, currency, invoicing schedule, expenses, and any tax or accounting assumptions. The parties should obtain transaction-specific legal and accounting advice on applicable requirements. No statutory tax, invoice, or payment rule is stated or allocated here.

<!-- section: ip -->
## 6. Background materials and project results — counsel completion required

Before work starts, counsel should prepare complete terms for each party’s pre-existing materials, project-specific source code, object code, designs, documentation, inventions, and other results. The parties must decide what is assigned, licensed, retained, excluded, or dependent on third-party rights, and confirm any required form or formalities under current law. This draft transfers no ownership and grants no license. Do not rely on a blank or informal IP arrangement.

<!-- section: third-party -->
## 7. Third-party and open-source components

The provider should identify third-party and open-source components, versions, licenses, notices, and material restrictions in a delivery inventory. The parties should agree on approval, replacement, maintenance, and disclosure steps after reviewing their actual dependency set and intended distribution. This section does not certify license compatibility or grant rights in third-party material.

<!-- section: confidentiality -->
## 8. Confidentiality

The parties should use a separate reviewed confidentiality agreement or insert a complete clause covering permitted use, access, exclusions, disclosures, safeguards, return or deletion, and duration. This draft does not create a complete confidentiality obligation. Coordinate any NDA with repository access, issue tracking, demonstrations, and third-party services.

<!-- section: data-security -->
## 9. Data, security, and subcontractors

Before a provider receives personal, production, or restricted data, document the actual parties, purposes, instructions, access, security measures, retention, incident process, subcontractors, and any required separate agreement. Verify current legal requirements and the actual processing facts with counsel; no CNDP procedure, filing, transfer rule, or legal role is asserted here. Prefer synthetic data for development and testing unless reviewed terms and safeguards are in place.

<!-- section: support -->
## 10. Deployment, maintenance, and support

The SOW should identify whether deployment, hosting, monitoring, maintenance, updates, support hours, response targets, backups, and handover are included or excluded. Any service level, service credit, continuity commitment, or security commitment requires complete terms reviewed for the project. No ongoing support is included merely because software is delivered.

<!-- section: assurances -->
## 11. Warranties, remedies, and liability — unresolved

Counsel should draft any representations, warranties, indemnities, liability limits, exclusions, and remedies after assessing the project and current mandatory rules. This discussion draft provides none and does not establish that any proposed allocation is available or enforceable.

<!-- section: term -->
## 12. Termination and transition — unresolved

The parties should decide with counsel how a project may be suspended or ended, how completed and in-progress work is handled, what amounts are due, what access is revoked, and whether transition assistance is needed. Applicable notice, cure, and post-termination consequences remain open and are not supplied by this draft.

## 13. Governing law, disputes, and general terms — unresolved

Counsel should complete governing law, forum or dispute process, notices, assignment, subcontracting, amendments, force majeure, severability, language, electronic execution, and order of precedence between this agreement and each SOW. No governing-law choice or dispute forum is selected here.
