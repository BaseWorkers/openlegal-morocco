## Exact-version review: Common Paper Mutual NDA

The [Mutual NDA Standard Terms Version 1.0](https://commonpaper.com/standards/mutual-nda/1.0) page identifies the version and release date (2021-07-19), says the version remains at that URL while later changes receive new version pages, and states the agreement is available under CC BY 4.0. The publisher says the license permits use and modification with attribution. The [CC BY 4.0 deed](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) describes attribution, link-to-license, modification notice, and no additional restrictions requirements.

The source registry records a narrow reuse authorization for the Standard Terms text on that exact version page, subject to preserving attribution and the license/link, linking the source, indicating changes, and not implying publisher endorsement or imposing additional restrictions. The authorization excludes the cover page, annotations, explanatory material, benchmark data, and other page content. Any resulting third-party wording must retain separate attribution and must not be presented as CC0 project content.

The publisher describes the agreement as U.S.-law-oriented and says its agreements were created with U.S. law in mind. No Moroccan legal suitability is inferred. No wording has been copied into OpenLegal; this record establishes the reviewed source scope and license terms only.

## Exact-file review: DINAcon standard-software draft

The inspected [DINAcon file at commit `e0617d5`](https://github.com/DINAcon/foss-legal-templates/blob/e0617d5554215bd77e4c55955c8bc5ea15603d9f/contract-software.de-CH.md) labels itself a Swiss software-contract draft, “Entwurf 0.1 — noch nicht freigegeben,” and states CC BY 4.0. GitHub's file history identifies this as an update dated 2024-11-15 and shows parent commit `23286e5`, dated 2024-09-25, with message “Initial contract from OSB Alliance.” The pinned file says it is based on the [OSB Alliance standard terms for transfer of standard software](https://osb-alliance.de/publikationen/veroeffentlichungen/standard-vertragsbedingungen). The publisher's [standard terms page](https://osb-alliance.de/publikationen/veroeffentlichungen/standard-vertragsbedingungen) and [same-day announcement](https://osb-alliance.de/pressemitteilungen/open-source-business-alliance-vereinfacht-geschaeftsbeziehungen-drei-standardvertraege-fuer-open-source) are dated 16 March 2015, state CC BY 3.0 DE for the contract texts, and specify the attribution “Copyright OSB Alliance e.V., Version 1/2015.” The announcement says the contract texts were formulated by JBB Rechtsanwälte with practical experience contributed by OSBA member companies; it does not identify the individual contributors or establish the provenance of later DINAcon edits.

The DINAcon repository landing page separately shows CC0-1.0. That repository-level signal does not resolve the individual file's different notice or the upstream license chain. The file is pinned to an immutable commit. GitHub records parent `23286e5` as “Initial contract from OSB Alliance” (2024-09-25); its commit metadata identifies the author and committer as account `loleg`, which does not establish legal authorship, assignment, title, or reuse permission. The parent file's title says standard software, while its opening clauses 1.1–1.2 describe SaaS/cloud use. The OSB Alliance has a separate [SaaS and cloud software PDF](https://osb-alliance.de/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/OSBA_Standard-Vertragsbedingungen_SaaS-_und_Cloudsoftware.pdf). On 2026-10-08, this publisher-hosted PDF was directly retrieved and visually reviewed in Chrome: it is 11 pages, the cover identifies SaaS/cloud, but the inner contract title identifies standard software while clauses 1.1–1.2 describe SaaS/cloud use. Its indexed text extraction shows clauses 1.1–1.2 with wording matching the DINAcon parent, supporting a likely direct source relationship but not proving the exact edition or complete textual identity. The PDF's 2020/04 upload path does not establish a revision, and no version marker appears on the inspected cover, inner title page, or final contact page. The publisher's 2015 page and announcement state CC BY 3.0 DE and specify the attribution “Copyright OSB Alliance e.V., Version 1/2015”; they do not establish that this internally inconsistent PDF is that exact edition. Exact source identity, authorship/contribution history, and compatibility of the repository, file-level, and upstream notices remain unresolved.

The 2024 update changes the earlier SaaS/cloud-oriented DINAcon draft into standard-software transfer terms (178 additions and 173 deletions in the commit diff). On 2026-10-08, direct retrieval of the OSB Alliance publisher's linked 12-page [standard-software PDF](https://osb-alliance.de/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/OSBA_Standard-Vertragsbedingungen_%C3%9Cberlassung_von_Standardsoftware.pdf) succeeded. Its visible title and extracted text identify standard-software transfer terms; clause 5.7 provides a two-week cure period and clause 12.1 uses “Textform,” while DINAcon's updated clause 5.7 uses four weeks and clause 12.1 uses “Schriftform.” These are examples, not a complete diff or legal/license compatibility conclusion. The PDF itself has no revision number or Version 1/2015 marker. The publisher's page dates the contract collection to 16 March 2015 and specifies the attribution “Copyright OSB Alliance e.V., Version 1/2015,” but the exact file history connecting the current PDF, the 2015 collection, and the DINAcon 2024 update remains unverified. No wording was copied. Do not treat the public repository, draft, or file notice as proof that this layered chain is cleared for this project.
