## CNDP website and cookie guidance — provision-level reconciliation

The CNDP's 11-page [website-compliance guide](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/CNDP-guide-conformite-sites-web-fr.pdf) describes itself as a practical, non-exhaustive guide based on Law 09-08 and its implementing texts. Sections 2–4 state that website controllers collecting personal data must notify the CNDP, describe authorization/declaration and foreign-transfer procedures, and direct sites to request consent at collection. Section 10 says websites using cookies that call on personal data must obtain the user's consent before deposit and explain their purposes and opposition means. These assertions are recorded as guidance text, not adopted template requirements.

CNDP Deliberation D-939-2025, dated 28 November 2025, addresses personal data processed by installing, recording, or storing cookies on a user's terminal. The five-page official PDF states a simplified-declaration model before implementation, lists purposes, says personal profiling requires prior authorization, sets a six-month retention cap, and distinguishes prior consent for personalized advertising and social-network sharing from the other listed purposes. Sections 6–7 also state pre-deposit information and security/subcontractor provisions, now recorded in proposition-level claims. It further states prior-authorization requirements for foreign transfers and certain interconnections. The bounded text claims are in `claims.json` as `cndp-deliberation-d-939-2025-cookie-*`. These are summaries of what the decision says, not conclusions about effect, currency, or application.

There is a direct textual difference between the older guide's broad cookie-consent instruction and D-939-2025's purpose-specific consent rule. This record does not determine whether D-939-2025 supersedes, clarifies, or operates alongside the guide, or resolve its formal publication/effect or current status. The decision's transfer statement also needs reconciliation with Law 09-08 Articles 12, 20, and 43–44. The guide, the decision, and the statutory text are recorded separately; no universal consent or CNDP filing rule is inferred for any particular setup. The Cookie Notice template does not select a user-control method and remains gated on technical facts and qualified counsel review.

1. Trace House proposal no. 272 (government report no. 200) after its 22 November 2024 committee transfer through later committee/plenary records and the Official Bulletin; then reconcile the Ministry database's “Aucune modification” entry for Loi 09-08 with the Official Bulletin and trace both the CNDP's 2013 draft amendments and its later institutional refonte project through any formal bill, parliamentary steps, publication, commencement, replacement, or abandonment. Separately trace the CESE's AI-related revision recommendation to any formal bill, parliamentary steps, Official Bulletin publication, and commencement rule. Establish current text/status for Decree 2-09-165 from authoritative sources as well.
2. Verify the relevant legal basis, information duties, rights, exceptions, security duties, retention, subcontracting, and transfer provisions against pinpointed primary texts.
3. Reconcile the directly verified mismatch between the CNDP procedures page's cookie-specific F-214 “normal declaration” label, the decision's “simplified declaration” text, and the accompaniment page's generic F214 “DS” label. Confirm the legal classification and current process with a qualified reviewer; do not generalize application across all processing.
4. For any sample notice, first define the actual organization, data subjects, purposes, data categories, collection points, recipients, processors, locations, retention, rights contact, and processing operations. A generic template cannot truthfully fill in those facts.
5. For a DPA, establish actual parties’ roles and instructions, security arrangements, subprocessors, incident workflows, transfers, and any separate CNDP action with counsel. The Articles 23–26 and 43–44 text checks in `claims.json` do not establish current law or case-specific procedure.
6. Obtain Moroccan legal and language review before presenting any privacy notice, cookie notice, or DPA as ready for reliance.

## Safeguards

- Do not import GDPR terminology or duties as if they were Moroccan requirements. Comparative material may only be used for issue spotting and must be identified as such.
- Do not claim that CNDP has approved a template or business practice.
- Do not publish guessed declaration, authorization, transfer, or breach-notification procedures or deadlines.
- The law and decree source records are promulgation texts, not proof of current consolidation. Current-law status remains unresolved until an amendment audit is complete.
