## Pinpointed text checks

- Article 7 says that the legal effect of a simple or advanced electronic signature as evidence before court cannot be rejected solely because it is electronic or does not meet qualified-signature requirements. This is not a conclusion that a particular signature is sufficient or reliable. See `law-43-20-simple-advanced-signature-evidence-article-7` in [`claims.json`](claims.json).
- Articles 7 and 84 were cross-checked between the Arabic SGG Official Bulletin No. 6951 and the French Official Bulletin No. 6970 reproduction hosted by DGSSI. The bounded wording aligns in the two copies. This is a text comparison only; it does not establish current application.
- Article 84 ties the law’s entry into force to publication in the Official Bulletin of implementing texts. This note does not establish when the trigger was satisfied. See `law-43-20-commencement-trigger-article-84` in [`claims.json`](claims.json).
- DGSSI's FAQ version-history table identifies version 1.1 dated 13 July 2023 and lists the law's entry into force and publication of requirement frameworks among its changes. The FAQ introduction separately says the law has applied since 13 July 2013. Its answer 2 states that Decree 2-22-687 was the only published application text for trust services at the time of writing, with technical requirement frameworks published on the authority's website. The 2013/2023 discrepancy and the full Article 84 trigger remain unresolved; neither the FAQ's metadata nor its time-bounded inventory is treated as a commencement determination. See `dgssi-faq-law-43-20-commencement-metadata-and-date-discrepancy` in [`claims.json`](claims.json).
- The six DGSSI requirements frameworks identified above each show an initial version 1.0 dated 13 July 2023. Ref_PSCo_AG describes itself as a provider compliance baseline and says the detailed frameworks complement it for distinct trust services. This confirms the existence and date of the technical publications named by the FAQ, but does not establish that their website publication is an Article 84 Official Bulletin publication or resolve how the statutory trigger applies. See `dgssi-trust-requirements-frameworks-publication-metadata` in [`claims.json`](claims.json).
- An Official Bulletin notice dated 16 April 2026 records a later individual DGSSI accreditation under Law 43-20 for a qualified electronic timestamp service; it says the decision's Arabic text appeared in Official Bulletin No. 7499 on 13 April 2026. This demonstrates a post-FAQ Official Bulletin publication made under the Law 43-20 framework. The notice is an individual accreditation rather than an identified general implementation instrument; it does not answer whether it satisfies Article 84's trigger or establish a commencement date. See `sgg-bo-7500-dgssi-trust-provider-accreditation-2026` in [`claims.json`](claims.json).
- Article 1 of Decree 2-22-687 identifies DGSSI under the National Defence Administration as the national authority for trust services under Law 43-20. See `decree-2-22-687-national-trust-authority-article-1` in [`claims.json`](claims.json).
- Article 37 states that the decree takes effect six months after publication in the Official Bulletin. The claim records the provision without calculating a date or addressing later changes. See `decree-2-22-687-entry-into-effect-article-37` in [`claims.json`](claims.json).
- The decree text refers to separately published requirements and standards for certain services. The DGSSI frameworks have now been inventoried at source-text level; this note does not determine their legal relationship to Article 84 or whether a particular service or signature method meets them.
