## Pinpointed text checks

- Article 1 of Law 31-08 describes objectives concerning consumer information and consumer contracts, including distance sales and solicitation. That objective statement does not establish that a particular product’s users are consumers or that any given Terms of Service is covered. See `law-31-08-objectives-consumer-contracts-distance-sales` in [`claims.json`](claims.json).
- The SGG consolidation’s Article 2 includes definitions of consumer and supplier; Articles 25–28 address distance contracts, including Article 28's listed exclusions for specified automated-machine, public-telephone, immovable-property, and public-auction contracts. The bounded Article 28 paraphrase is recorded as `law-31-08-distance-contract-chapter-exceptions-article-28` in [`claims.json`](claims.json). These provisions need Gazette cross-checking and fact-specific analysis before a business applies them. The consolidation is dated 22 May 2025 and carries the SGG authority caveat recorded in [`claims.json`](claims.json).
- Article 29 lists distance-offer disclosures and requires clear, understandable information with its commercial nature unambiguous; it also describes pre-contract order choices and confirmation or modification. Article 30 addresses access to remote-supply contractual terms and express consumer acceptance before offer acceptance. Articles 32–34 address written/durable-medium confirmation, unsolicited paid supply, and proof in disputes. Bounded paraphrases are recorded as `law-31-08-distance-offer-information-and-order-review-article-29`, `law-31-08-distance-contract-terms-access-and-acceptance-article-30`, and `law-31-08-distance-sale-written-confirmation-article-32`, `law-31-08-unsolicited-paid-supply-no-silence-as-acceptance-article-33`, and `law-31-08-supplier-burden-of-proof-distance-sale-article-34` in [`claims.json`](claims.json). These are not determinations that a particular offer or acceptance flow is covered or legally sufficient; Gazette cross-checking and fact-specific review remain necessary.
- Article 36 states seven full days to exercise withdrawal, extended to 30 days if the supplier fails to provide the specified written confirmation under Articles 29 and 32. The period runs from receipt of goods or, for services, acceptance of the offer. Articles 38 and 42 qualify that rule: Article 38 lists withdrawal exceptions, and Article 42 excludes certain contract categories from Articles 29, 32, 36, and 37. The bounded timing claim is `law-31-08-distance-contract-withdrawal-period-article-36` in [`claims.json`](claims.json); it does not determine whether an exception applies to a transaction. Current law remains unresolved pending Gazette and amendment checks.
- Article 37 says a supplier must refund the full amount paid immediately and within 15 days after withdrawal, with the legal rate of interest applying after that period. The text says this rule is subject to Articles 38 and 42. See `law-31-08-distance-contract-withdrawal-refund-article-37` in [`claims.json`](claims.json); the timing and remedy remain unreviewed and current-law status unresolved pending Gazette and amendment checks.
- Article 38 lists five categories where withdrawal may not be exercised unless the parties agree otherwise: early-started services with consumer consent; market-fluctuation-priced supplies; custom, non-returnable, or perishable goods; accessed audio/video recordings or software; and newspapers, periodicals, or magazines. See `law-31-08-withdrawal-exceptions-article-38` in [`claims.json`](claims.json). These are paraphrases, not determinations that a particular product or service fits a category; Article 42 exclusions are separate.
- Article 42 excludes Articles 29, 32, 36, and 37 for regular doorstep supply of ordinary consumer goods by distributors on frequent, regular rounds, and for accommodation, transport, restaurant, or leisure services booked for a specific date or period. Its final paragraph retains Articles 29 and 32 for electronic contracts involving those listed services. See `law-31-08-distance-contract-scope-exclusions-article-42` in [`claims.json`](claims.json); whether a real arrangement fits these categories remains a fact-specific legal question.
- Article 39 sets a 30-day maximum for fulfilling an order, measured from the day the supplier confirms receiving the consumer's order, unless the parties agree otherwise. The bounded text claim is `law-31-08-order-fulfilment-deadline-article-39` in [`claims.json`](claims.json); chapter coverage and any contrary agreement require separate analysis.
- Article 40 addresses non-performance because the requested product, goods, or service is unavailable: the supplier must inform the consumer and, where applicable, refund amounts paid immediately and within 15 days after payment; legal-rate interest applies after that period. See `law-31-08-unavailable-order-notice-and-refund-article-40` in [`claims.json`](claims.json). The refund qualification and transaction-specific application remain unresolved.
- Article 41 permits a same-quality, same-price substitute if that option was disclosed before contracting or included clearly and understandably in the contract. If the consumer exercises withdrawal in that situation, the supplier bears return costs and must inform the consumer. See `law-31-08-equivalent-substitute-and-return-costs-article-41` in [`claims.json`](claims.json); equivalence and disclosure remain fact-specific.
- Article 43 states that, notwithstanding contrary legislation, the supplier alone bears responsibility in an intellectual-property dispute. The text-level claim is `law-31-08-supplier-ip-dispute-responsibility-article-43` in [`claims.json`](claims.json). Its scope and interaction with intellectual-property statutes require qualified interpretation; no case-specific conclusion is recorded.
- Article 44 states that the provisions of this chapter are matters of public order. The text-level claim is `law-31-08-distance-contract-chapter-public-order-article-44` in [`claims.json`](claims.json); its consequences and scope have not been interpreted here.
- Article 45 begins the next chapter and describes sales outside commercial premises, including proposals made at a natural person's home, residence, or workplace (even at the person's request), and sales in places not intended for shopping, including supplier-organized meetings or trips. The bounded scope claim is `law-31-08-off-premises-sales-scope-article-45` in [`claims.json`](claims.json); it does not decide whether a particular transaction is covered.
- Articles 46–48 provide chapter exclusions and contract formalities. Article 46 lists three excluded activity/product categories. Article 47 calls for a written Article 45 contract, delivery of a counterpart at conclusion, and a detachable Article 49 withdrawal form, with handwritten signing and completion of all counterparts; the form's details are set by regulation. Article 48 lists required contents under penalty of nullity and prohibits a jurisdiction-assignment clause. See `law-31-08-off-premises-sales-exclusions-article-46`, `law-31-08-off-premises-written-contract-article-47`, and `law-31-08-off-premises-contract-content-article-48` in [`claims.json`](claims.json). Implementing rules, scope, translation nuances, and legal consequences remain unreviewed.
- Articles 49–52 address withdrawal and telephone sales. Article 49 states a seven-day withdrawal period from the order or purchase commitment, exercise by sending the detachable form through a means proving receipt, and nullity of a waiver; it excludes contracts under Article 51. Article 50 bars demanding or obtaining payment, an undertaking, or service before the period expires and requires return of obligations/payment orders within 15 days after withdrawal. Article 51 requires identity and commercial-purpose disclosure for telephone or similar off-premises sales, offer confirmation, and consumer signature for the offer to bind. Article 52 classifies the chapter's provisions as public order. Bounded claims are `law-31-08-off-premises-withdrawal-right-article-49`, `law-31-08-off-premises-payment-restriction-article-50`, `law-31-08-off-premises-phone-sale-article-51`, and `law-31-08-off-premises-chapter-public-order-article-52` in [`claims.json`](claims.json). The rules' application, implementing details, and legal effects remain unresolved.
- Articles 53–54 address discount sales. Article 53 defines a discount sale by reference to prior or accompanying advertising, an announced aim to clear stocked goods rapidly, and a price reduction. Article 54 requires a clear, readable discount announcement and, at the point of sale, identification of covered goods, current and crossed-out prices, and the sale period. Its reference-price rule caps the crossed-out price at the lowest actual price charged for a similar item at the same establishment in the prior 30 days; discount percentages may also be stated. See `law-31-08-discount-sale-definition-article-53` and `law-31-08-discount-advertising-and-reference-price-article-54` in [`claims.json`](claims.json). The SGG consolidation and the ACAPS-hosted Official Bulletin reproduction provide text-level cross-checks; interpretation, amendments, and application remain unresolved.
- Law 78.20, published in Official Bulletin No. 6945 on 21 December 2020, replaces Law 31-08 Article 202. Its text assigns subject-matter jurisdiction in supplier-consumer disputes exclusively to the court of first instance despite a contrary clause, and gives the consumer a choice of territorial venue between the consumer’s domicile or residence and the place of the act causing damage. Article 2 states Law 78.20 takes effect on publication. The claim `law-78-20-amends-law-31-08-article-202` records a bounded paraphrase of the Arabic Gazette text cross-checked against the SGG consolidation; subsequent amendment status and qualified interpretation remain unresolved. This enacted amendment is separate from the SGG-listed draft Law 13-23.
- Articles 65-4 and 65-5 of the Law 53-05 copy dated 2021-03-18 address electronic offers and certain order-review/confirmation steps in that text. They are not a conclusion about current law, service scope, exceptions, or how a specific clickwrap should operate.
- Law 43-20 and implementing texts require separate review before any claim about e-signature type, trust services, validity, or proof is made.

The response matrix refers to proposed draft Article 172-1. Its appearance on the SGG site establishes a public-comment-stage proposal existed, not its later legislative status. A pinpointed status lead is tracked in `claims.json`; current law remains unresolved.

## Open issues before a Terms of Service template can be relied on

1. Retrieve the ministerial answer associated with House written question 24323's 10 December 2025 answer-date field and trace the SGG-listed draft Law 13-23 through parliamentary records. Verify any resulting instrument against Official Bulletin publications, cross-check relevant Law 31-08 provisions, and verify implementing rules.
2. Confirm the current consumer definition and analyze whether a particular product is B2C, B2B, or mixed. Do not assume a single set of terms fits all three.
3. Identify the actual sales/service model, price display, order steps, confirmation, cancellation/refund policy, renewals, and consumer support before drafting user-facing claims.
4. Reconcile the Law 53-05 copy dated 2021-03-18 with Law 43-20, Decree 2-22-687, and later Official Bulletin changes. Determine which rules apply to the real offer and acceptance flow.
5. Review consumer, payment, distance-sales, digital content/service, advertising, and dispute provisions against primary current text and qualified Moroccan advice.
6. Have Moroccan counsel and language reviewers approve the completed terms; no generator use or release is enabled at this draft stage.

## Safeguards

- Do not claim that a click, checkbox, email, or electronic signature is legally sufficient without an authoritative, current, fact-specific analysis.
- Do not insert cancellation, refund, renewal, warranty, or liability rules as mandatory law until supported by current primary sources.
- Keep B2B, B2C, and mixed-audience applicability explicit and counsel-reviewed.
- Record any later amendment and change the affected claims’ current-law statuses only after verifying the text and scope.
